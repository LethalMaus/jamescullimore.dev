Room to Grow: Walking Through the Gardens of Android Databases

By James Cullimore • Feb 7, 2024 • 9 min read

Have you ever felt as if you were navigating a maze when handling the database for your Android app? So have a seat on this virtual park bench, for today we’re venturing into the wonderful world of Jetpack Room, where data storage is a breeze.

Consider this scenario: a clever Android developer who, bored with typical database difficulties, releases the power of Room to simplify the entire process. Let us discover the mysteries of Room while strolling through the greenery of our codebase.

In the first half of our trip, we’ll look at the best circumstances for adopting Room, identifying the sweet spots where it shines the brightest. Whether you’re working with intricate data interactions or just need a dependable means to store and retrieve data, Room has you covered.

But wait — there’s more! We’ll take a scenic diversion to investigate alternate options, ensuring you have a well-rounded understanding of database administration. Sometimes it’s not just about what works, but what works best for your unique requirements.

Migrations might be difficult to traverse, but worry not, our intrepid coder has perfected the art. We’ll go over the complexity of conducting and testing migrations to ensure your database matures seamlessly with your app.

Before we wrap off our tour, our developer colleague will reveal some secret tips and tactics for taking your Room experience to the next level. So, let us go on this trip together, as the universe of Room unfolds before us, one code snippet at a time.

When the Room shines brightest

Imagine Room as your trusty sidekick, ready to shine when your Android app needs a robust, yet elegant, solution for data storage. The beauty of Room reveals itself in scenarios where structured and persistent data management is crucial. Think of it as your go-to companion when dealing with complex data relationships, intricate queries, and a desire for a clean, object-oriented API.

Room thrives when you’re dealing with datasets that have clear patterns, making it the perfect fit for storing entities with well-defined relationships. Whether it’s user profiles, product details, or any other piece of your app’s puzzle, Room excels at providing a seamless storage solution.

One of Room’s standout features is its ability to double as an offline cache. Picture this: your user is on a mountain hike, far away from the realms of a stable internet connection. With Room, your app can cache relevant data locally, ensuring a smooth user experience even in the absence of a network. This offline resilience makes Room particularly handy for apps that demand consistent functionality regardless of connectivity.

However, every superhero has its kryptonite. Room might not be your go-to solution if you’re dealing with non-relational data or require a database that scales horizontally across multiple servers. In situations where a lightweight, NoSQL database might be more fitting, exploring alternatives could be the key to a more tailored solution. Room, after all, is the hero of structured data, but every hero has its domain.

Everyone has their own Room

While Room stands tall in the realm of structured data on Android, there are alternative options that cater to different needs and scenarios. Let’s take a stroll through the landscape of database alternatives, each with its unique charm.

Shared Preferences

For lightweight, key-value pair storage needs, Shared Preferences emerges as a swift and simple choice. Ideal for storing small pieces of data like user preferences or app settings, Shared Preferences offers a quick and accessible storage solution. However, its simplicity comes with limitations — it might not be the hero you need when dealing with large datasets or complex relationships.

NoSQL Solutions

For those venturing into the world of non-relational databases, NoSQL options like Firebase Realtime Database or MongoDB become compelling choices. These alternatives are well-suited for scenarios where flexibility in data structure is key. NoSQL databases shine when dealing with unstructured or rapidly evolving data. Just be mindful that the trade-off often involves sacrificing some of the transactional consistency provided by traditional relational databases like Room.

SQLDelight for Kotlin Multiplatform

Now, if you’re exploring the vast realms of Kotlin Multiplatform, SQLDelight emerges as a powerful contender. It seamlessly integrates with both Android and iOS platforms, allowing you to share your database logic across different platforms. This compatibility, coupled with the benefits of a type-safe SQL abstraction, makes SQLDelight an excellent choice for projects with shared codebases.

However, keep in mind that SQLDelight might not be the silver bullet for every use case. If your app primarily targets only Android, Room might provide a more Android-centric experience. It’s all about weighing the trade-offs and choosing the solution that aligns best with your project’s requirements and the platforms you’re targeting. As we meander through the options, each alternative unveils a different facet of database management, offering developers a diverse toolkit to tackle various challenges.

Furnishing your Room

Now that we’ve explored the scenic views of database alternatives, let’s roll up our sleeves and dive into the practical side of Room. Setting up Room is a breeze, and with Kotlin by our side, the journey becomes even more enjoyable.

Setting up Room in your Android Project

Dependencies: Ensure you have the necessary dependencies in your app’s build.gradle file. Include the Room dependencies, and don’t forget the Kotlin extensions for Room.

implementation "androidx.room:room-runtime:2.6.1" kapt "androidx.room:room-compiler:2.6.1" implementation "androidx.room:room-ktx:2.6.1"

Database Class: Create a class that extends RoomDatabase. This class serves as the main access point to your app’s persisted data.


                        @Database(entities = [YourEntity::class], version = 1, exportSchema = false) abstract class YourAppDatabase : RoomDatabase() {
                        abstract fun yourDao(): YourDao
                        }

DAO (Data Access Object): Define the DAO interface to interact with your entities. Annotate your queries and methods accordingly.


                        @Dao interface YourDao {
                        @Query("SELECT * FROM YourEntity")
                        suspend fun getAllEntities(): List
                        @Insert(onConflict = OnConflictStrategy.REPLACE)
                        suspend fun insertEntity(entity: YourEntity)
                        }

Initialization: Initialize your database instance. Consider using a singleton pattern to ensure a single database instance throughout your app.


                        val database = Room.databaseBuilder( applicationContext,
                        YourAppDatabase::class.java,

"your_database_name").build()

Moving out of your Room

When the winds of change sweep through your app’s data layer, migrations become a crucial aspect to ensure a smooth transition without leaving users stranded in a data limbo. Let’s embark on a step-by-step guide on how to gracefully handle migrations in Room, accompanied by Kotlin code examples. Moreover, we’ll shed light on writing tests to safeguard against potential pitfalls that might arise from a broken migration.

Handling Migrations in Room

Update Entity: If your data structure changes, modify the existing entity class to reflect these alterations. For example, if you’re adding a new field:


                        @Entity data class YourEntity(
                        // existing fields
                        val oldField: String,
                        val newField: String // added field
                        )

Increment Database Version: In your YourAppDatabase class, increment the version number to signal that a migration is needed.


                        @Database(entities = [YourEntity::class], version = 2, exportSchema = false) abstract class YourAppDatabase : RoomDatabase() {
                        // ...
                        }

Create Migration: Build a Migration class to handle the transition from the old to the new database schema.


                        val migration_1_2 = object : Migration(1, 2) { override fun migrate(database: SupportSQLiteDatabase) {
                        // SQL statements to migrate data
                        database.execSQL("ALTER TABLE YourEntity ADD COLUMN newField TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT ''")
                        }
                        }

Apply Migration: Incorporate the migration when building your database instance.

                        YourAppDatabase::class.java,

"your_database_name").addMigrations(migration_1_2).build()

Maximize your Room

As we navigate through the Room landscape, let’s uncover some extra tips and tricks that will empower you as a developer, making your journey smoother and more enjoyable.

Use Transactions Wisely

Leverage transactions for bulk operations. Encapsulating multiple database operations within a transaction ensures atomicity, maintaining data integrity and improving performance.


                  database.runInTransaction {

// Perform multiple database operations}

Optimize Queries with @Transaction

When dealing with multiple queries in a single method, annotate the method with @Transaction to optimize performance. Room will treat the entire method as a single transaction, reducing the overhead of multiple transactions.


                        @Transaction suspend fun complexOperation() {
                        // Multiple database queries or operations
                        }

Fine-Tune Query Performance

Keep an eye on your queries’ performance by using tools like the Android Profiler. Additionally, Room provides EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN to analyze the execution plan of a query, helping you optimize when needed.

Utilize Room’s LiveData and Flow Integration

If your app follows a reactive paradigm, take advantage of Room’s built-in support for LiveData (AndroidX) or Kotlin Flow. This ensures your UI stays automatically updated when underlying data changes.

@Query("SELECT * FROM YourEntity") fun getAllEntities(): LiveData>

Implement Type Converters Effectively

Room’s type converters are handy when dealing with non-trivial data types. Create custom converters to seamlessly convert complex objects or structures into types that Room understands.


                        @TypeConverter fun fromJson(json: String): YourCustomObject {
                        // Convert JSON string to YourCustomObject
                        }
                        @TypeConverter
                        fun toJson(customObject: YourCustomObject): String {
                        // Convert YourCustomObject to JSON string
                        }

Consider Database Pre-Population

If your app requires an initial set of data, consider pre-populating the database. Room allows you to include a pre-packaged database file, simplifying the setup for users.

Watch Out for LiveData Observers

Be cautious with LiveData observers in your UI. Ensure proper lifecycle management to prevent memory leaks, especially when observing in fragments or activities.

Optimize Migrations for Large Datasets

When migrating large datasets, consider breaking down migrations into smaller steps to avoid performance issues during the migration process.

Explore Room Database Inspector

Use the Room Database Inspector in Android Studio to visually inspect and debug your app’s database. It provides valuable insights into the structure and contents of your database.

Stay Updated on Room Releases

Keep an eye on Room library releases for new features, optimizations, and bug fixes. Staying up-to-date ensures you benefit from the latest improvements.
